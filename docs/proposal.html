Product proposal · v0.4 · November 2026

iAmUnemployed

An English-language community for unemployed people, built around a leaderboard that works like a meme. We laugh at the situation, back each other up, and celebrate loudly when someone escapes.

Your number 147 days in the queue

The core idea: unemployment is lonely and draining. iAmUnemployed turns it into something you share. You count your days like a number in a queue, earn ridiculous titles, and make cards for social media. When you land a job, you become an Escapee and help the people still in line.

Why English from day one

The audience is many times larger: the US, the UK, Ireland, Canada, Australia, and everyone in Europe who job-hunts in English. The meme also already exists. TikTok has thousands of "Day X of being unemployed" videos. The app gives that trend a home, a counter and a community.

Greek and other languages arrive in phase 3. That is why the code keeps every piece of text in translation files from the start.

The heart of the app

The leaderboard as a meme

The leaderboard is a running joke. It works like a "days without an accident" sign or a gaming rank: the more days you rack up, the more over-the-top your title gets. The real glory sits with the Escapees, though. That is why there are three boards instead of one.

How it goes viral

Shareable cards

Every moment becomes a square card, ready for TikTok, Instagram, X or LinkedIn. The day count always appears in the queue-display typeface, so the cards are recognizable at a glance, the way Wordle results are.

iAmUnemployed · DAY 147 The ATS rejected me before a human could. Honestly, respect the efficiency. PhD in Iced Coffee#iAmUnemployed
UNICORN OF THE WEEK "Entry level. 5+ years experience. Must speak 4 languages. Salary: competitive." 412 votes#unicornjobs
iWasUnemployed · 214 DAYS I'm out. Thank you, queue. Now I'm coming back for the rest of you. Escapee #1,284#iescaped

Rejection Bingo

A monthly bingo card: "we'll keep your CV on file", "overqualified", "we're looking for someone with passion". Complete a line and share your card.

Unicorn of the Week

Members post absurd job ads and vote. The winner becomes the meme of the week. Company names are blurred automatically.

Unemployment Wrapped

Like Spotify Wrapped: "83 applications, 6 interviews, ~140 iced coffees". It comes out when you escape or at the end of the year.

Creator templates

Ready-made templates for the existing "Day X of being unemployed" trend. The hashtag and the counter advertise the app for free.

The reward

I Escaped: the moment you land a job

Tap I escaped! and a small ceremony begins. The counter stops, the queue display turns green and the community hears the news.

  1. Confetti and an escape card. Your profile flips from iAmUnemployed to iWasUnemployed. The card shows your total days, your escape number ("Escapee #1,284") and your Wrapped, ready to share.
  2. The Wall of Escapees. A permanent place in the Hall of Fame. Other members leave congratulations, like signing a birthday card.
  3. A gift from a sponsor. For example, a discount on clothes for your first day, your first week of coffee, or a course subscription. This comes in phase 2.
  4. New role: Mentor. You stay in the community with the Escapee badge. You answer questions, review CVs and run mock interviews.
  5. Referrals. Flag "my company is hiring" and recommend members. Every hire through one of your referrals earns you the rare Liberator badge.

If the job doesn't work out, tap "I'm back" with zero shame. The counter starts over, the Escapee title stays forever, and you earn The Sequel badge.

The community

The feed: thoughts and experiences

Categories

Mental Health · Interviews · CVs & Résumés · Rejections · Benefits & Paperwork · Career Change · Wins · Memes.

Anonymous posts

Everyone posts under a nickname, and a "fully anonymous" option covers the heaviest topics.

Accountability buddy

Get paired with someone in your field and time zone. A weekly check-in: what I did, what I'll do next.

City and industry groups

London, Dublin, New York, Toronto, plus groups by industry (tech, design, hospitality). The same system covers Athens later on.

Daily check-in

One mood emoji and one move for the day (an application, a course, a coffee chat, a workout). This is what feeds Grind of the Week.

Resources by country

Benefits and free training differ from country to country. Members set their country and see what applies to them. The community helps keep the lists current.

Boundaries

Rules of the humor

The meme is the growth engine and also the biggest risk. Without rules, the app could turn toxic or hurt someone who is already struggling.

  • Laugh at the situation, never at people. Jokes aimed at specific members get removed.
  • The leaderboard is opt-in. Counters are private by default. You join the public board only if you choose to, and always under a nickname.
  • No company or personal names in job-ad memes. Names are blurred automatically and posts are reviewed before they go live. Defamation law varies by country and is especially strict in the UK.
  • A "Not in the mood for jokes today" button. It hides memes and leaderboards and shows only supportive content.
  • Crisis detection. If a post signals despair, the helpline for the member's country appears quietly and a moderator is alerted.
  • Human moderation across time zones. Volunteer Escapees at first, then paid moderators.

Helplines are shown based on the member's country. For example:

User journey

From day one to the escape

  1. Sign-up in 30 seconds. Nickname, industry, country and city, and "since when". You instantly get your queue ticket with your day count and your first title.
  2. First card. The app suggests "Share your Day 1". This is the first viral moment.
  3. Daily routine. A check-in, a look at the feed, and one move to keep the streak alive.
  4. Weekly rhythm. Monday: a fresh Grind of the Week and a check-in with your buddy. Friday: the Unicorn of the Week vote.
  5. The escape, the ceremony and the switch to mentor. The loop closes and feeds the next people in line.
Technical

How it's built

Platform

Start with a web app (PWA) that opens on a phone with no install. Cards are shared with a link. Build a native app (React Native / Expo) only once demand is proven.

Backend

Supabase or Firebase for accounts, the database, the real-time feed and image storage. Low cost at the start.

Languages (i18n)

Every piece of text lives in translation files (for example next-intl or i18next) from day one. Launch is English only, so the Greek version becomes a translation job instead of a rewrite.

Meme cards

Generated on the server as images (for example with Satori / OG images). Each card has its own link with a clean preview on social media.

Escape verification

Runs on trust, since no money is involved. Once sponsor gifts arrive, ask for a simple confirmation, such as an email from a company domain.

Time zones

Grind of the Week resets on Monday in each member's local time, so nobody loses a streak because of the clock.

Plan

  • English only, ready for i18n
  • Sign-up and day ticket
  • Titles and 3 leaderboards
  • Shareable cards
  • Feed with categories
  • The escape and the Wall
  • Rules and helplines by country

A community that sticks

  • Buddies and city groups
  • Badges and Rejection Bingo
  • Unicorn of the Week
  • Wrapped
  • First gift sponsors
Phase 3 · scale

More languages, more jobs

  • Greek, Spanish, German
  • Mentors and referrals
  • Employers on the platform
  • Native app

First test, before writing any code: post 10 meme cards on TikTok and Instagram under the "Day X of being unemployed" trend and the #iAmUnemployed hashtag, linking to a landing page that says "Get your queue number". If a few hundred people sign up within two weeks, the idea has momentum.

Identity

The name: iAmUnemployed

The name says exactly what the app is and works as a hashtag, so every shared card carries it. It also flips when someone lands a job: their profile changes from iAmUnemployed to iWasUnemployed.
